Open interest vs volume

Every option chain shows two activity numbers side by side: volume and open interest. They sound similar but measure different things, and comparing them is one of the simplest ways to spot new positioning.

The difference

  • Volume is the number of contracts traded today. It resets to zero every morning and updates through the day.
  • Open interest (OI) is the number of contracts that are still open, counted after the previous day's close. It updates once a day.

Every trade adds to volume. Open interest only changes when a trade opens or closes a position: if a new buyer and a new seller trade, OI rises by the size of the trade; if both sides are closing, OI falls; if one side opens and the other closes, OI stays the same.

Why compare them

When today's volume in a contract is much larger than its open interest, most of that volume must be new trading rather than existing positions changing hands. This volume-to-open-interest ratio is one of the main checks behind "unusual options activity" screens. Tomorrow's open interest then shows how much of today's volume stayed open.

Liquidity

High volume and high open interest usually come with tighter bid-ask spreads, so it costs less to get in and out. Contracts with little of either can have wide spreads and stale last prices.

Questions

Why is open interest the same all day?

Exchanges publish open interest once a day, based on the previous close. Volume updates live.

Can volume be higher than open interest?

Yes. When volume is much higher than open interest, the contract is seeing a lot of new trading that day.
