How to read an option chain

An option chain lists every call and put for a stock, grouped by expiry and ordered by strike. Once you know what each column means, the chain tells you how the market prices risk, where traders are positioned and how liquid each contract is.

Layout

Most chains show calls on the left and puts on the right, with the strike prices down the middle. You choose one expiry at a time. Strikes near the current stock price are at the money; calls below and puts above the stock price are in the money.

The columns

ColumnMeaning
Bid / AskThe best price buyers are paying and sellers are asking. You usually buy near the ask and sell near the bid.
LastThe price of the most recent trade. It may be stale for contracts that rarely trade; the bid and ask are more current.
VolumeContracts traded today.
Open interest (OI)Contracts still open from previous days. Updated once a day.
Implied volatility (IV)The volatility the option price implies, as an annual percentage. Higher IV means more expensive options.
Delta (Δ)How much the option price moves for a $1 move in the stock, and a rough probability of finishing in the money.

What to look for

Liquidity

Narrow bid-ask spreads, high volume and high open interest mean you can get in and out at fair prices. Index ETFs like SPY and QQQ have the most liquid chains.

Positioning

Strikes with large open interest show where traders hold positions. Large volume today at a strike with little open interest shows new positioning.

The volatility smile

Plotting IV against strike usually gives a smile or a skew: out-of-the-money puts often carry higher IV than calls because investors pay for crash protection. A sudden rise in IV at one strike or expiry often comes before an event.

Put/call ratios

Dividing put volume (or open interest) by call volume gives a quick read on positioning for that expiry.

Questions

Why is the last price different from the bid and ask?

The last price is the most recent trade, which may have happened minutes or hours ago. The bid and ask show the current market.

What is the difference between volume and open interest?

Volume counts contracts traded today. Open interest counts contracts that remain open from previous days and is updated once a day.
